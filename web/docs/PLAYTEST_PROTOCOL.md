# First-time Expedition playtest protocol

## Status and recruitment

No independent participants were available during development of Expedition 0.3. Agent-driven browser sessions and deterministic policies are engineering evidence, not human feedback. Target five independent first-time players, with at least two using a phone if available. Obtain consent before recording; use anonymous session IDs and avoid collecting personal data.

## Conditions

Use the same verified commit and record browser, viewport/device, input type, art style, motion preference, and whether audio is enabled. Start a fresh page on Canyon with default credits, 1× speed, no prior instruction beyond “Try this game as if you found it online. Say what you are thinking.” Allow the player to choose controls and pacing. Do not explain counters, income, or wave calls before they encounter them.

## Session (15–20 minutes)

1. Ask “What do you think your objective is?” Record their answer verbatim.
2. Start the timer when Begin mission becomes usable. Observe the first successful deployment; record seconds, selected defender, attempts on occupied/acid cells, and whether they discover the crew cards or keyboard.
3. Observe opening spending. Ask after the decision “What made you buy that?” Record which income units they notice and what they expect them to do. Do not treat a particular strategy as correct.
4. At the first Tank encounter, observe whether plating and the suggested counters are understood. Ask “What seems different about this enemy?” Record whether the answer comes from the field, warnings, unit details, or guide.
5. When Call next wave becomes available, ask “What would happen if you used that?” Record understanding of credits, the three-second lead, surviving enemies, and future schedule shift before they click.
6. Let them finish or fail. Note pauses, replacements, salvage, losses, confusion, long idle periods, and self-reported boring or overwhelming moments. Record the mission time of each observation.
7. Ask them to restart or try another destination. On a phone, ask them to inspect the far-right field and select the Falcon without explaining scrolling/zoom. Observe panning, targeting mistakes, warnings, landscape layout, and reachability.
8. Ask whether they want another mission and why. Ask for the most confusing moment and their favorite decision. Avoid leading questions about the new features.

## Synthesis

Keep observations separate from interpretations. Report each issue's frequency out of completed sessions, severity (blocked, costly/repeated confusion, minor friction), and concrete evidence. Five sessions can find usability problems; they do not establish population win rates or statistical enjoyment claims. Retest fixes with new players when possible. Do not silently merge agent runs into participant counts.
