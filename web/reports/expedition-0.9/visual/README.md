# Sprinter and challenge review evidence

- [Animated walk loop](sprinter-walk.gif): 24 shipped poses, approximately 1.116 seconds per cycle, composed at 128×96 world canvas plus 2.5× detail against all three environments. The torso is fixed in place for pose review; this is not recorded gameplay. Left is the retained illustration, not a claim that the old presentation had no procedural bobbing.
- [Unedited real-combat recording](real-combat.webm): Chromium public-control encounter, six purchased Asteroid barriers, actual spawned/attacking Sprinters, 2×, pause, Original/Expedition switching, reduced motion and three restarts. No private simulation manipulation. Setup and lifecycle checks remain in the recording. The recorded viewport is 1280×720; focused overview deliberately scales the field down to fit, so creatures appear smaller than their world-space review above.
- [Real combat screenshot](real-combat.png): fourteen live enemies, two Sprinters contacting barriers in separate lanes, existing health/damage effects and adjacent overlapping aliens.
- [Individual pose sheet](poses.png): selected walk, idle and attack frames enlarged for joint/silhouette inspection. These are scaled production cells, not high-resolution source components.
- [Hard desktop](hard-desktop.png) and [Hard phone layout](hard-phone.png): opt-in briefing/current difficulty. Phone is viewport emulation, not a physical device.

Source-resolution parts and lossless packed poses are in [art-source/expedition-0.9](../../../art-source/expedition-0.9/). The development-only [animation fixture](../../../tests/fixtures/animation-lab.html) demonstrates walk/idle/attack, pause, 1×/2× and representative reduced-motion poses across all backgrounds. It is excluded from the production entry, and its controlled preview clock is not a second game/combat clock.

Reproduce the GIF/pose sheet with `python3 scripts/research/visuals-0.9.py`. Record the public-control scenarios with `AVA_RECORD_ANIMATION=1 npm run test:e2e -- --project=chromium tests/browser/expedition-0.9.spec.ts`; pass the resulting test-output directory to the Python script to copy the unedited combat capture. Standalone ffmpeg on this host is an incompatible executable, so no video transcoding workaround or extra dependency was introduced.

## Agent visual review

Individual raw components, assembled poses and the loop show actual alternating thigh/shin/foot placement, not whole-image motion. Foot alpha bounds stay within one source pixel of the 105px ground reference; perspective places the far foot slightly higher. The common head/torso/tail layer preserves crest, amber eye, coral spots and teal shading. Arms counter-move during walking. Contact starts with a six-world-pixel lunge/arm extension; recovery and next-strike anticipation follow the authoritative cooldown. Idle is restrained and distinct; it is mainly visible in the fixture because ordinary enemies walk or attack.

The first small-cap assembly exposed the body socket and oversized arms; it was rejected. The selected pivot moves inside the thigh muscle and overlaps the hip. Separate full-frame generation was not repeated after 0.3's rejected repaint/flicker experiment. The nine-part runtime-container approach was assessed but not implemented or benchmarked; no unsupported performance comparison is claimed.

The derivative's hips are more compact than the static source and the tail tip differs slightly. These are acknowledged pilot differences, not an exact tracing. At battlefield scale its color/crest/eye/biped identity remains recognizable, joints stay connected and the loop is readable; enlarged detail is softer. At very short desktop heights the existing overview shrinks all characters. The pilot is suitable for a single-character trial, not evidence to replace all aliens. Dense-motion tolerance, appearance preference and attack readability still require genuine first-time-player observations. Agent review is not independent human approval.
